🗣️

Language is a tool

I combine the communicative approach with comprehensible input. Students use English for real-life topics like their hobbies, dreams and future careers, not only to memorize rules.

💛

Mistakes are celebrated

Young learners need to feel safe before they speak. I use warm language to lower the affective filter, and I use stories, videos and real interactions so grammar comes from context.

⏱️

Students do the talking

I keep teacher talk to about 20% of the lesson. Every class moves from warm-up to modeling, then guided practice, then independent practice with peers while I give feedback.

🎭

Real-life assessment

Progress shows in real tasks: songs, poetry, presentations, role-plays, month-long drama projects, and even a field trip where students shop in English with real people.

Reading · Boost Up 4, Unit 7

What do you do in your free time?

Students learn to talk about their free time, turn the question back with "What about you?", and answer naturally with "Nothing much" or "Same as usual."

Lesson highlights
  • Model: a "find your match" flashcard hunt, using gestures, until students take over as the questioner.
  • Guided: listen to the dialogue, answer comprehension questions, read chorally, then fill in the gaps from memory.
  • Independent: pair role-plays. Confident pairs swap partners with students who need support until the whole class can do it.
  • Assessment: an unaided role-play in front of the class.

Writing · Boost Up 6, Unit 7

In the future

Students talk about their future selves (jobs, where they'd like to live) and practice "Can I give it a try / a shot / a go?". Then they write a short paragraph about what they want to do and why.

Lesson highlights
  • Model: students' own answers become a chart on the board, which they use as a writing toolbox.
  • Guided: dialogue listening, a gap-fill from memory, then two model paragraphs to show what's expected.
  • Assessment: anonymous writings go up on the monitor for class-wide peer feedback on what works and why.

Speaking · Phonics · Oxford Phonics World 2

"sc" and "sk" sounds

Students learn to pronounce and spell words with sc and sk (scared, scooter…) and use them in simple sentences. Wordwall games and flashcards keep the drilling fun.

Lesson highlights
  • Model: the new words are introduced on Wordwall and drilled with word search and whack-a-mole.
  • Guided: each student makes a sentence with their flashcard, or passes it to a friend for help.
  • Independent: groups of four compete to produce the most sentences.
  • Assessment: everyone stands up and shares two sentences using flashcards of their choice.

Listening · Boost Up 2

At home

A song-based listening lesson. Students listen to a short text and answer questions about it, sing it from memory, and reuse the language (hungry, thirsty…) in their own sentences.

Lesson highlights
  • Two-pass listening: first for the gist and questions, then again, pausing at the exact moment each answer appears.
  • Guided: students fill in the missing words of the dialogue orally.
  • Independent: a "song battle". One student sings a line and their partner continues from there.
  • Assessment: a front-of-class challenge to crown the "king of songs".

Grammar · Boost Up 2

Where are you?

Students ask and answer "Where are you? / Where is he?" and "What are you doing?" using places like bathroom, pool, garden.

Lesson highlights
  • Model: every student holds a location flashcard, and the teacher walks around "looking for friends", calling each one by name.
  • Guided: students take turns asking classmates, with a pronunciation focus on what and where.
  • Assessment: students answer a random question chosen by their classmates, with no help from the teacher.

Pronunciation · Boost Up 2, Unit 7

It's dinner time!

Students act out a family dinner dialogue with gestures, focusing on clear pronunciation of words like wash, time, kids and chunks like "It's … time" and "I am so …".

Lesson highlights
  • Warm-up: "What did you have for dinner yesterday? Who made it?"
  • Model: the teacher performs the role-play with gestures, and the whole class stands up to copy the words and movements.
  • Independent: pairs rehearse, and strong pairs mix with students who need support.
  • Assessment: workbook pp. 64–65, then a final performance judged on pronunciation accuracy.

Listening · Boost Up 2

My favorite animal

Students talk about their favorite animals with "I like…" and "How about you?", practice short and long vowels, and finish by writing and performing their own dialogue.

Lesson highlights
  • Warm-up: a phonics review race, an animal-sound guessing game, zoo stories, and a spider-web mind map on the board.
  • Model: the teacher plays an animal looking for its animal friends.
  • Pronunciation focus: short o, i, e compared with long o‑e, i‑e, e‑e.
  • Assessment: students write a dialogue on their own and perform it with no help.

▶
Reading · Boost Up 6, Unit 7

Superheroes (reading)

Students read a text about spiders, the "real superheroes" of nature, and discover that animals and people have superpowers too. The lesson uses both intensive and extensive reading techniques.

Lesson highlights
  • Warm-up: a random-picker app on a smartphone chooses who answers, so everyone stays alert.
  • Model: students skim, raise a finger for each unknown word, then teach each other the new vocabulary.
  • Intensive: Wordwall vocabulary games, reading along line by line with the audio, and a gap-fill.
  • Extensive: pairs brainstorm superheroes and their powers, then present them.

Writing · Boost Up 6, Unit 7

Superheroes (writing)

Students write a paragraph about superpowers in their own voice. They choose a tone (comedy, sci-fi or shōnen) and write it on school tablets using PollEv.

Lesson highlights
  • Pre-writing: the class builds a shared word bank on the board, and the success criteria are made explicit.
  • Guided: three model texts, one in each tone, and noticing the grammar students will need.
  • Post-writing: every submission appears on the monitor, the class discusses strong points, and repeated grammar errors are addressed together.

Speaking · Real-world task

The shopping mall

Students ask prices and give opinions: "How much is this?", "It looks amazing on you!", "That color doesn't suit you." Then they use the language with real shopkeepers on a field trip.

Lesson highlights
  • Model: the teacher goes "shopping" around the room, using facial expressions and gestures.
  • Independent: a shopping competition in groups of three. The group that gets the most items for the least money wins.
  • Assessment: the "In the City" field trip, with real conversations with real people.

Teacher tips

Tip 1

A Pokémon point system for class management

ClassDojo · PNG images · under 1 minute per class

Students earn points on ClassDojo for finishing activities, answering questions and good behavior, and lose points for disruptions. What keeps them chasing points is the rewards:

  1. 100 pts — they hatch an egg and get their first monster
  2. 200 pts — they pick their own favorite Pokémon as their avatar
  3. +100 pts — their Pokémon evolves, one stage at a time
  4. Max evolution — every extra 100 pts earns a real Pokémon card

The rewards are close enough together to feel achievable, and there are enough stages to keep students motivated all year. When students watch their own Pokémon evolve on the classroom screen, good behavior stops being a rule and becomes something they care about.

Tip 2

Gamify vocabulary with Wordwall

wordwall.net · images · 5–10+ minutes

Students are often tired after morning classes, and vocabulary is where they tune out. Wordwall turns a word list into matching games, quizzes and word searches. You can play them on the classroom monitor or send them home as a link.

Images give new words something to attach to, and the game format asks for more than memorizing. Students have to remember, answer quickly, and move to click, point or shout the answer. Add points or rewards and they will keep repeating the words without zoning out.

Money-saving tip: ask your school to buy one shared account for all the teachers, or split it with colleagues.
